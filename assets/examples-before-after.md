# Examples: Before & After

Annotated rewrites. The pattern to notice: the "after" versions are not longer
or louder - they are more specific, and they answer something.

---

## Example 1 - Chef's knife

### Before

> **Premium Chef's Knife - 8 Inch**
>
> This premium quality knife is crafted from high-quality stainless steel and
> features an ergonomic handle designed for comfort and precision. Perfect for
> any kitchen, this versatile knife will make food preparation easier and more
> enjoyable. Buy now!

**What is wrong**

- Hook is the category, not a transformation.
- "Premium quality" / "high-quality" - banned openers, mean nothing.
- "Ergonomic handle designed for comfort" - no spec, no felt consequence.
- "Perfect for any kitchen" - universal claim, unprovable.
- Bare "Buy now" CTA.
- No Details block; a shopper cannot find the steel, the angle, or the care.

### After

> **Slices a ripe tomato without crushing it**
>
> A 15° edge on 61 HRC Japanese steel - sharp enough to cut clean on contact,
> hard enough that you are not sharpening it every weekend. Balanced at the
> bolster, so a full night of prep does not ache your wrist.
>
> - **Holds an edge for months** - 61 HRC core steel resists rolling, so you
>   sharpen about twice a year instead of twice a month.
> - **Cuts on contact, no sawing** - a 15° per-side edge glides through tomato
>   skin and onion layers without crushing them.
> - **Won't rust in a salt-air kitchen** - 316 stainless with a full tang, sealed
>   at the bolster where cheaper knives wick water.
> - **Balanced for long prep sessions** - weight sits at the bolster, so your
>   wrist stays neutral through an hour of chopping.
>
> **Good to know:** It is a thin 15° edge - do not use it on bone or frozen
> food, or you will chip it. Hand wash and dry; the dishwasher dulls the edge
> and can spot the finish.
>
> Get a knife that still slices clean a year from now.
>
> **Details**
>
> | Spec | Value |
> | --- | --- |
> | Blade length | 8 in (203 mm) |
> | Steel | 316 stainless, 61 HRC |
> | Edge angle | 15° per side |
> | Construction | Full tang, sealed bolster |
> | Handle | Pakkawood, riveted |
> | Weight | 7.4 oz (210 g) |
> | Care | Hand wash, dry immediately |
> | Warranty | Limited lifetime |

**Why it works**

- Hook names the felt outcome and stands alone.
- Every bullet is spec → consequence, with a distinct payoff (edge, cut, rust,
  comfort) rather than four versions of "durable."
- The objection note gives a real limitation - a chip risk - which reads as
  honesty and prevents returns.
- Details block carries every number; no marketing language in it.
- CTA restates the benefit.

## Example 2 - Linen shirt

### Before

> **Classic Linen Shirt**
>
> Made from the finest linen, this timeless shirt offers effortless style and
> breathable comfort. A wardrobe essential that never goes out of fashion.
> Available in multiple colors.

**What is wrong**

- "Finest linen" - unverifiable superlative, no GSM.
- "Effortless style," "timeless," "wardrobe essential" - filler.
- "Breathable comfort" - true of all linen, says nothing about *this* shirt.
- No sizing honesty, no shrinkage, no care, no measurements.

### After

> **The shirt that gets softer every summer instead of thinning out**
>
> Woven from 165 GSM European flax - heavy enough to drape instead of cling,
> light enough to wear through a humid afternoon. Cut with a relaxed shoulder
> so it works open over a tee or buttoned on its own.
>
> - **Softens with every wash** - 165 GSM long-fibre flax breaks in without
>   pilling, so year three fits better than year one.
> - **Drapes instead of clinging** - the heavier weave hangs away from the body
>   in humidity rather than sticking to it.
> - **Room through the shoulder** - a relaxed cut and a box pleat at the back
>   yoke, so you can reach without pulling.
> - **Washes cold and line-dries flat** - no dry cleaning, no ironing if you
>   shake it out.
>
> **Good to know:** Runs large - if you are between sizes, size down. Expect
> about 3% shrinkage on the first wash in length only; the chest and shoulder
> do not move. The white is unlined, so it is semi-sheer in direct sun.
>
> The shirt you will still be reaching for in five summers.
>
> **Details**
>
> | Spec | Value |
> | --- | --- |
> | Fabric | 100% European flax linen, 165 GSM |
> | Fit | Relaxed; runs large |
> | Chest (size M) | 43 in (109 cm) |
> | Length (size M) | 29 in (74 cm) |
> | Shrinkage | ~3% in length, first wash |
> | Care | Machine wash cold, line dry, warm iron |
> | Closure | Mother-of-pearl buttons |
> | Origin | Woven in Portugal |

**Why it works**

- The hook sells the aging behavior, which is the actual reason people buy linen.
- GSM gives the "finest" claim something to stand on.
- "Runs large" and "semi-sheer in direct sun" cost a few carts and save the
  returns that actually hurt.
- Care is written in words, not symbols.

## Example 3 - Bluetooth speaker (what not to claim)

### Before

> **Portable Bluetooth Speaker**
>
> Experience audiophile-grade sound with deep bass and crystal clarity. 24-hour
> battery life. Waterproof. The best portable speaker on the market.

**What is wrong**

- "Audiophile-grade," "crystal clarity" - subjective, unprovable.
- "24-hour battery" - at what volume? Unstated conditions make it useless.
- "Waterproof" - needs the IP rating and scope.
- "Best on the market" - comparative with no baseline; often disallowed.

### After

> **Loud enough for a backyard, small enough for a daypack**
>
> A 40 W class-D amp and a passive radiator push real low end at 80 dB without
> the box rattling on the table. Twenty hours at 50% volume - a full day of
> music, not a full afternoon.
>
> - **Fills a patio without distorting** - 40 W class-D with a rear passive
>   radiator holds clean output up to 85 dB.
> - **Lasts a full day out** - 20 hours at 50% volume, 6 hours at max.
> - **Survives the pool and the rain** - IP67: dust-tight, and 30 minutes
>   submerged at 1 m.
> - **Pairs with two phones at once** - Bluetooth 5.3 multipoint, so you and a
>   friend can both queue tracks.
>
> **Good to know:** IP67 covers water immersion, not salt water - rinse with
> fresh water after beach use. Battery figures are at 50% volume at 20°C; cold
> weather shortens them.
>
> A speaker that is still playing when the sun goes down.
>
> **Details**
>
> | Spec | Value |
> | --- | --- |
> | Output | 40 W class-D |
> | Drivers | 2 × 45 mm full-range + passive radiator |
> | Frequency response | 55 Hz - 20 kHz |
> | Battery | 20 h at 50% volume / 6 h at max |
> | Charging | USB-C, 3 h to full |
> | Water resistance | IP67 (dust-tight, 30 min at 1 m) |
> | Bluetooth | 5.3, multipoint, 30 m range |
> | Dimensions | 7.5 × 3.1 × 3.1 in |
> | Weight | 1.6 lb |
> | Warranty | 2 years |

**Why it works**

- Every subjective claim replaced with a measured one, with test conditions.
- "IP67" instead of "waterproof" - and the note says what IP67 does *not* cover.
- Battery stated at two volume levels, because "24 hours" without conditions is
  meaningless.
- No superlative survived, and none was needed.

---

## The pattern across all three

1. **The hook names a felt outcome**, never a category.
2. **Every vague adjective is replaced by a number or a mechanism.**
3. **The limitation is stated plainly.** Honesty is the strongest trust signal
   available, and it also prevents returns.
4. **Details block carries the proof**, clean of selling.
5. **Nothing got longer for its own sake** - the "after" versions are specific,
   not verbose.
