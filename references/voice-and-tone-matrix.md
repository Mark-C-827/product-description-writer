# Voice & Tone Matrix

Voice is the brand's personality; tone is its mood in a given sentence. PDP copy
needs the voice matched and the tone calm - the shopper is deciding, not being
entertained.

## Calibration axes

Score the brand 1-5 on each before writing:

| Axis | 1 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal ↔ casual | "Formulated for" | "Works on" |
| Reserved ↔ warm | "Effective cleansing" | "The kind of clean you notice" |
| Technical ↔ plain | "61 HRC, 15° edge" | "Stays sharp" |
| Serious ↔ playful | "Engineered for precision" | "Cuts like it means it" |
| Minimal ↔ expressive | Short declaratives | Sensory, descriptive |

## Category defaults

Use when the brand voice is unknown. Always flag the assumption.

| Category | Voice | Sentence length | What it avoids |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premium skincare | Calm, precise, unhurried | Medium | Hype, exclamation, "miracle" |
| Mass beauty | Friendly, direct, sensory | Short | Clinical detachment |
| Consumer electronics | Concrete, confident, plain | Short | Adjectives over numbers |
| Audio / audiophile | Technical, specific, a little reverent | Medium | Vague sound claims |
| Apparel (premium) | Understated, material-forward | Medium | Trend slang |
| Apparel (casual / street) | Playful, conversational | Short | Formality |
| Outdoor / gear | Blunt, capability-first | Short | Lifestyle fluff |
| Tools / hardware | Terse, spec-forward | Very short | Emotion |
| Home / furniture | Warm, spatial, tactile | Medium | Abstraction |
| Food / beverage | Sensory, appetite-first, warm | Short | Nutrition-speak |
| Supplements / wellness | Careful, factual, no outcome claims | Short | Any implied cure |
| Baby / kids | Reassuring, safety-forward | Short | Cleverness at the cost of clarity |
| Jewelry | Precise on materials, romantic on meaning | Medium | Vague "luxury" |
| Auto parts | Exact, compatibility-first | Very short | Personality |

## The one calibrating question

When voice is unknown, infer from category, then ask exactly one question.
Pick the one that unblocks the most:

1. "Should this read more like a specialist explaining it, or a friend
   recommending it?"
2. "Is the brand trying to feel premium, or accessible?"
3. "If this page had a temperature - cool and minimal, or warm and
   conversational - which?"

One question. Not a survey. If they do not answer, build to the category
default and flag the assumption in "Notes to merchant."

## Tone rules that outrank voice

These hold regardless of brand:

- **Declarative beats exclamatory.** One exclamation point per page, at most,
  and usually zero.
- **Second person.** "You get" not "customers get."
- **Active voice.** "Sealed seams stop leaks" not "leaks are prevented by
  sealed seams."
- **Concrete nouns.** "Salt air" not "harsh environmental conditions."
- **No hedging stack.** "Might possibly help" → say what it does.
- **No emoji in body copy** unless the brand already uses them everywhere.
- **Consistency.** Do not start playful and end in spec-sheet deadpan.

## Voice kills clarity - resolve in favor of clarity

If a voice choice makes a shopper work to understand the product, cut it.
Playful is fine; playful that hides the size is not. A shopper who laughs and
then cannot find the dimensions will not buy.

## Worked contrast - the same knife, three voices

**Premium / reserved**
> Slices a ripe tomato without crushing it. A 61 HRC core holds a 15° edge
> through months of daily use, so sharpening becomes an annual task rather than
> a weekly one.

**Casual / playful**
> Cuts a ripe tomato clean - no sawing, no squashed mess. The steel holds its
> edge for months, so you sharpen it about twice a year and forget about it.

**Technical / terse**
> Tomato-clean cuts at 15°. 61 HRC core steel, edge retention ~6 months under
> daily use. Sharpen twice yearly.

Same specs, same benefit, three brands. The facts do not change; only the
distance to the reader does.

## Checklist

- [ ] Voice matches the brand (or the flagged category default)
- [ ] Sentence length matches the target
- [ ] Tone is consistent top to bottom
- [ ] No exclamation stacking
- [ ] Second person, active voice
- [ ] Voice never costs clarity
