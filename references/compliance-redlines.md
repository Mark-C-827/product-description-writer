# Compliance Redlines

The hard boundary: **write only what the source proves.** Unsupported claims on
a PDP are not just a copy problem - they are refunds, chargebacks, platform
penalties, and in regulated categories, legal exposure.

## The governing rule

If you cannot point to the line in the source that supports a claim, you cannot
write the claim. When asked to state an outcome the source does not support:
**refuse, and offer a compliant alternative.**

That last part matters. "I can't claim that, but here's what I can say" is
useful. A bare refusal is not.

## Claim categories and what each requires

| Claim type | Requires | If missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Performance number (speed, capacity, battery hours, load rating) | A stated test condition and result | Describe the mechanism, not the number |
| Comparative ("2× faster than") | A named baseline and test | Drop the comparison; state absolute spec |
| Certification (OEKO-TEX, CE, FCC, UL, Energy Star) | The actual cert name and scope | Omit entirely - never imply |
| Material composition | Stated material and % | Use the material name only if certain |
| Origin ("made in", "Italian leather") | Verified sourcing | Omit |
| Health / medical outcome | Clinical evidence **and** category legality | Do not write. Ever. |
| Skin claims ("non-comedogenic", "hypoallergenic") | Test result or cert | Describe what it excludes instead |
| Safety ("child-safe", "non-toxic") | Standard met and named | State the design feature, not the safety conclusion |
| Sustainability / "eco" | Specific, verifiable basis | Name the actual attribute (recycled content %) |
| Earnings / business outcomes | Never appropriate on a PDP | Omit |
| Endorsements / "as seen in" | Real placement | Omit |
| Scarcity / urgency | It must be true *now* | Omit |

## Safe rewrites

| Unsupported | Compliant alternative |
| --- | --- |
| "Clinically proven to reduce wrinkles" | "Formulated with 0.3% retinol; patch test before full use." |
| "Lasts forever" | "61 HRC steel - sharpen about twice a year under daily use." |
| "Dermatologist tested" | "Fragrance-free and alcohol-free." |
| "Eco-friendly" | "68% recycled cotton; undyed." |
| "Non-toxic, safe for kids" | "Water-based finish; conforms to EN 71-3." *(only with the cert)* |
| "The best knife you'll ever own" | "A 15° edge that holds through daily use." |
| "Ships in 24 hours" | Omit unless fulfillment actually guarantees it. |
| "Thousands of happy customers" | Omit unless you have the number and can source it. |

## Regulated categories - extra caution

- **Health / supplements / wellness:** no diagnosis, treatment, prevention, or
  cure claims. No outcome promises. Structure/function language only, and only
  where lawful.
- **Cosmetics:** no drug claims (acne treatment, collagen regeneration) unless
  the product is regulated as one.
- **Children's products:** safety claims require the named standard. Never
  improvise an age rating.
- **Food:** allergen and nutrition statements must match the label.
- **Electronics:** no unverified regulatory marks; no interference or safety
  claims without the cert.
- **Automotive:** no warranty or emissions claims without documentation.

## Urgency and scarcity

Allowed when true: limited batch, seasonal availability, genuine low stock,
real deadline.

Never fabricate: countdown timers, "only 3 left" without a real number,
fake restocks, perpetual "sale ends tonight."

If the merchant asks for urgency and there is none, say so. Offer a real
alternative risk-reducer instead - warranty, returns, trial.

## Price and offer claims

- Do not state a discount the source does not support.
- Do not state "free shipping" unless the policy is real and unconditional.
- Do not imply a limited-time offer that is permanent.

## Pre-ship pass

Before delivering, check each sentence:

1. Does every number trace to the source?
2. Does every claim of superiority name what it is better than?
3. Is every certification named and real?
4. Are there any health / safety / earnings implications? If yes, cut or prove.
5. Is any urgency or scarcity real and current?
6. Any claim I would be uncomfortable defending to a customer who was
   disappointed? Cut it.

Log every claim you could not support in "Notes to merchant," with the
compliant alternative you used instead. That list is the single most valuable
part of the deliverable - it is where the merchant's real risk lives.
